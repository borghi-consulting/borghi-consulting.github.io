INSIGHTS — Diversification

What does diversification mean in crypto?

By Alessandro Borghi and Giulia D’Agostino · Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 19 November 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer

Diversification means spreading your money across different assets, so that one of them failing does not decide the whole outcome. In crypto it is harder: the market is concentrated in a few large assets, most react to the same shifts in sentiment, and risks such as custody and scams never show up on a price chart.

Key points

  • Diversification spreads exposure so that one asset, sector or provider failing has a smaller effect on the whole.
  • It reduces the impact of individual failures. It does not remove risk or guarantee any outcome.
  • In crypto, holding many tokens is not the same as being spread out: market value is concentrated, and most assets react to the same changes in sentiment.
  • Risk is not only price. Where and how holdings are kept, and exposure to scams, matter as much.
  • A structure that gives every holding a defined job, such as the five blocks of the Hive Block Strategy™, shows how your holdings are spread and why. It is a way of mapping, not a recommendation.

What does diversification mean?

Diversification means spreading your money across a number of different assets, so that one of them performing badly has a smaller effect on the whole. ASIC’s Moneysmart, the Australian regulator’s consumer site, describes spreading across types of assets, sectors, countries and styles.

The aim is not to avoid every loss. It is that a single company collapsing, one sector going flat or one country’s market falling does not take everything with it. Moneysmart notes that losses in one place might be offset by stronger results elsewhere. That is a possibility, not a guarantee.

Two terms come up often. Volatility is how much a value moves up and down. Rebalancing is bringing a mix back to the shape you chose after prices have moved it. Both come back below.

Why is diversification harder in crypto?

Because the usual building blocks do not carry over cleanly. Four things get in the way.

  • The market is concentrated. The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) noted in 2022 that more than 16,000 crypto-assets existed, but that market value was heavily concentrated in a small number of larger ones. Holding many tokens does not by itself mean being spread out.
  • Assets react together. The RBA described the market as highly interconnected and sensitive to changes in risk sentiment. It also found that unbacked crypto-assets such as Bitcoin and Ether had become more correlated with shares since the start of the pandemic. Different names can respond to the same shift.
  • Attention moves prices. Moneysmart lists media focus, public announcements and large holders promoting an asset on social media among the factors that can move a price.
  • There are no standard categories. Moneysmart points out that each crypto-asset has different features and that there are no universally defined categories. Shares and bonds come with familiar labels; crypto does not.

These are descriptions of the market in published sources, not forecasts. The RBA figures date from 2022, so look for current data before relying on them.

Is diversification only about price?

No. In crypto, how and where holdings are kept can matter as much as what they are.

Moneysmart warns that many crypto providers are not licensed, so you may not be protected if a platform fails or is hacked, and that a stolen wallet is very hard to get back. The RBA likewise describes cyber-attacks as prevalent and recourse for stolen or lost crypto-assets as minimal.

That is why a spread across assets alone can leave a gap. Holdings that all sit on one platform, behind one password or one set of keys share a single point of failure. Moneysmart notes that a hardware wallet, kept offline, may offer more protection. Scams are the other side of the same risk: Moneysmart says scammers use crypto because transactions are hard to recover. How do you keep a diversified mix in balance?

By reviewing it from time to time against the shape you set, and deciding in advance what happens when a holding drifts.

Moneysmart explains why: values change in different ways, so a balanced mix drifts over time. If one part rises while another falls, more of the total ends up in the part that rose, and the overall risk is likely to rise with it. Rebalancing means adjusting back to the mix that suits your goals and your appetite for risk. Moving money between holdings can have tax consequences, so speak to a registered tax professional before you act.

What can a structure not do?

It cannot remove risk, guarantee an outcome or tell you which assets to hold.

Moneysmart is direct about crypto: the value is very volatile, and you should be prepared to lose everything you put in. A structure does not change how volatile the underlying assets are. What it changes is how clear and consistent your own decisions are: every holding has a reason, a limit and a review rhythm, and you know in advance what you will do when something goes wrong.

If your situation involves tax, a company, a trust or an estate, ask a licensed professional in your country. The rules differ from place to place.

Frequently asked questions

Is holding many different tokens the same as being diversified?

No. If most of them react to the same shifts in sentiment, or all sit on the same platform, one event can still affect all of them. The RBA noted in 2022 that crypto market value is heavily concentrated in a small number of larger assets.

Does diversification protect me from losing money?

It can reduce the effect of one holding failing, but it does not remove risk or guarantee any outcome. Moneysmart says crypto-assets are high-risk and very volatile, and that you should be prepared to lose everything you put in.

Are stablecoins a safe way to diversify?

Not automatically. “Stablecoin” describes an aim to hold a steady value, not a guarantee. The RBA noted in 2022 that redemption is not legally guaranteed for some stablecoins and that algorithmic designs are inherently fragile. Moneysmart calls the word a marketing term.

How many crypto assets do I need to be diversified?

There is no correct number. What matters more is the job each holding does, how the holdings relate to one another, and how and where they are kept.
